## Systems
- **baseline**, conventional turn-based RAG:
  - waits for utterance end, then sends one query (the full utterance)
  - dense-only retrieval, no reranker, no decomposition, no suppression
  - follow-ups are handled by concatenating the previous utterance and restarting
  - same synthesizer and validator as the proposed system
- **proposed**, Streaming Live RAG: learned controller, early provisional retrieval, decomposition, hybrid retrieval, facet filter, reranker, fusion, refinement and suppression
- **ablations** (each is the proposed system with one change): `dense_only` (no BM25, no reranker), `hybrid_no_rerank`, `no_decomposition`, `rule_controller`, `no_early_retrieval`

## Metric definitions
- **Recall@k:** fraction of gold intents with at least one acceptable gold section in the top-k fused evidence of the turn. For refinement turns, the delta gold is used.
- **Citation hit rate:** fraction of gold intents for which the final answer cites an acceptable gold section. This is the answer-level quality measure.
- **Early retrieval rate:** among turns that need retrieval and have ≥ 2 chunks, the fraction whose first `RETRIEVAL_STARTED` precedes `UTTERANCE_END`.
- **False-trigger rate:** fraction of no-retrieval turns (presentation or chit-chat) with any retrieval call.
- **Multi-intent identification:** a compound turn counts if there are ≥ 2 sub-queries and each gold intent is matched by the top-3 of a *different* sub-query.
- **Citation support rate:** supported claims divided by all claims, according to the grounding validator.
- **Abstention accuracy:** a fully unanswerable turn must contain no supported claims; a partially unanswerable turn must flag the missing part in `uncertainty`.
- **TTFT:** time from `UTTERANCE_END` to the first `ANSWER_DELTA` (validated claim), measured on one monotonic clock.

**Reading the latency rows honestly:**
- The proposed system does more work per turn than the bare baseline: cross-encoder reranking of chunks and sentences, and several sub-queries. Its TTFT is therefore **185 ms vs 15 ms** for the baseline.

- In other words, early retrieval moves most of the heavy work into the time the user is still speaking.

### Analysed edge cases
1. **Topical but unanswerable questions.** The sufficiency gate is a single threshold on the best sentence-level cross-encoder logit. Questions that share many words with the corpus but ask for something absent can clear it and receive related, non-responsive sentences. The claims stay grounded (they are true corpus text) but do not answer the question.
2. **Paraphrased presentation requests.** Requests unlike the 84 training utterances can be classified as refinements. This triggers one unnecessary retrieval and reshapes nothing. It is the one false trigger in the table above.
3. **Compound requests answered by sibling sections.** The decomposer separates them only when the best sections differ. Near-identical phrasings merge by design, and some legitimate two-part questions merge too.

All of these are listed in `docs/limitations.md`.
